# Legitimacy and epistemic adequacy

Legitimacy asks whether an actor is entitled to decide. Epistemic adequacy asks whether the decision process can access the knowledge materially required to decide within its declared scope. These properties are independent: a decision can be legitimate, transparent, and procedurally complete while remaining epistemically inadequate.

| Property | Testable question | Primary evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | Who may decide, under what mandate and scope? | authority record |
| Policy | Which rules constrain the decision? | policy reference |
| Receipt | What inputs and reasoning entered the decision? | decision receipt |
| Epistemic adequacy | Could relevant situated knowledge reach and challenge the decision? | epistemic impact assessment |
| Enforcement | Was the resulting effect admitted, suspended, modified, or denied? | effect-status receipt |

The repository therefore composes epistemic evidence with existing decision receipts rather than redefining receipt semantics.
